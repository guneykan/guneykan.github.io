I am a research scientist interested in optimization, sampling, quantum computing, and algorithms more broadly. I currently work as a quantum algorithms researcher at JPMorgan Chase in New York.

I am particularly interested in the mechanisms behind quantum algorithms and in understanding when, if at all, they offer an advantage over classical algorithms. Comparing quantum dynamics with analogous classical dynamics helps me understand how these algorithms evolve and where differences in performance arise.

As a theoretical computer scientist, I care most about the practical implications of theory and about understanding how quantum algorithms actually work. I approach claims of quantum advantage with skepticism, looking for mechanisms that classical algorithms cannot easily reproduce and asking whether they make running a quantum computer worthwhile.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from Penn State in 2025 ([dissertation](https://etda.libraries.psu.edu/catalog/23460gmo5119)). I also have a background in physics and software engineering.
